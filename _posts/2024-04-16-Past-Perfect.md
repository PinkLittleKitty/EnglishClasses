## 12A - Past Perfect

The past perfect tense is a verb form used to refer to a past action that occurred before another past action.

### How do we make the Past Perfect?

<div class="grammar-box" markdown="1">
<span class="formula">Subject + had + Past Participle (e.g., "I had run")</span>

*   All verbs in the past perfect take this form regardless of the subject (e.g., “she had known,” ”we had known”).
*   For **negatives**, use **had not** (or **hadn't**).
*   For **questions**, place **had** before the subject.
</div>

| Sentence Type | Affirmative | Negative (Contracted) | Interrogative |
| :--- | :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **I** | I had eaten | I hadn't eaten | Had I eaten? |
| **You** | You had eaten | You hadn't eaten | Had you eaten? |
| **He/She/It** | He had eaten | He hadn't eaten | Had he eaten? |
| **We** | We had eaten | We hadn't eaten | Had we eaten? |
| **They** | They had eaten | They hadn't eaten | Had they eaten? |

---

### How to use the Past Perfect?

The past perfect tense is used:
*   To describe a past event that occurred **prior to another past event**.
*   To talk about time up to a certain point in the past.
*   In conditional sentences (3rd conditional) to talk about unreal past events.

> [!NOTE]
> Think of it as the **"Past of the Past"**.

**Examples:**
*   *Kora and Jill **had worked** together for years before they became friends.*
*   *When I got home, I realized I **had left** my keys at the office.*
*   *Nobody noticed that the chef **had burned** the turkey.*

---

### Present Perfect vs. Past Perfect

| Tense | Usage | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Present Perfect** | Past action with present consequences. | "I **have eaten** there before." |
| **Past Perfect** | Past action before another past action. | "I **had eaten** there many times before it closed." |

---

### Simple Past vs. Past Perfect

*   **Past Simple**: Actions completed in the past.
*   **Past Perfect**: Actions that occurred **before** another past action. Sentences often use the Past Simple to set the second "anchor" point.

---

### Forming Negatives & Questions

<div class="grammar-box" markdown="1">
*   **Negatives**: *Aaron was tired because he **had not** eaten all day.*
*   **Questions**: *Had the bus left by the time you arrived?*
*   **Wh- Questions**: *Where **had** the pirates **hidden** the treasure?*
</div>
