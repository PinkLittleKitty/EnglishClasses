<div class="grammar-box" markdown="1">
Prepositions of movement show movement from one place to another. They **always describe movement** and are usually used with **verbs of motion** (go, run, drive, fly, etc.).
</div>

### Common Prepositions of Movement

| Preposition | Meaning / Usage | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **To** | Direction of something. | *He drove **to** London.* |
| **Across** | From one side to the other. | *The dog ran **across** the road.* |
| **Around** | Passing something in a curved route. | *They walked **around** the town.* |
| **Away from** | Point where movement begins. | *The mouse ran **away from** the cat.* |
| **Down** | Higher point to a lower point. | *They ran **down** the hill.* |
| **Up** | Lower point to a higher point. | *Jack and Jill ran **up** the hill.* |
| **Into** | To an enclosed space / contact. | *He got **into** the car.* |
| **Out of** | Enclosed space where it begins. | *He went **out of** the room.* |
| **Off** | Away/down from something. | *The glass fell **off** the table.* |
| **Onto** | To the top surface of something. | *They went **onto** the stage.* |
| **Over** | Above and across the top. | *The cat jumped **over** the wall.* |
| **Past** | Moving in front of something. | *We drove **past** the school.* |
| **Through** | In one side and out the other. | *The train goes **through** a tunnel.* |
| **Towards** | In the direction of something. | *She walked **towards** him.* |
| **Under** | Directly below something. | *The mouse ran **under** the chair.* |
