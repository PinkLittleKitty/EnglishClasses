## 8B - Murphy’s Law (Conditionals)

### First Conditional
The first conditional is used to talk about a **possible condition** and its **probable result** in the future.

<div class="grammar-box" markdown="1">
#### Uses of the First Conditional
We use it to talk about plans and realistic possibilities. 
*   *If I save enough money, I will go to the beach.* (Si ahorro suficiente plata, voy a ir a la playa.)
*   *If she passes the exam, she will travel abroad.* (Si ella aprueba el examen, viajará al extranjero.)
</div>

---

### Structure
The first conditional has two clauses: the **condition** and the **result**.

<span class="formula">If + Subject + Present Simple, Subject + Will + Infinitive</span>

**Affirmative Form:**
*   *If my family buys the land, we will build a house.*
*   *We will build a house if my family buys the land.*

**Negative Form:**
Use *don't* / *doesn't* in the "if" clause and *won't* (will not) in the result clause.
<span class="formula">If + Subject + don't/doesn't + Present Simple, Subject + won't + Infinitive</span>
*   *If you don’t drive carefully, you will have an accident.*
*   *You will have an accident if you don’t drive carefully.*

**Interrogative Form:**
Reverse the order of the clauses.
<span class="formula">Will + Subject + Infinitive + if + Subject + Present Simple?</span>
*   *Will you buy me an ice cream if I clean my room?*
*   *Will he pick you up if it is too late?*

---

### Zero vs. First Conditional

| Tense | Meaning | Structure |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Zero Conditional** | Facts / General Truths. | <span class="formula">If + Present Simple, ... + Present Simple</span> |
| **First Conditional** | Probable future results. | <span class="formula">If + Present Simple, ... + Will + Infinitive</span> |

*   **Zero**: "If this happens, that happens." (Si esto pasa, eso pasa).
*   **First**: "If this happens, that **will** happen." (Si esto pasa, eso va a pasar).
