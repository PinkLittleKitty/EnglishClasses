### Phrasal Verbs & Word Order
Phrasal verbs consist of a **verb** plus one or two **particles** (prepositions or adverbs).

<div class="grammar-box" markdown="1">
#### Meaning Shift
The particles can change the meaning of the verb significantly:
*   **Bring**: To carry something.
*   **Bring up**: To raise/educate a child.
*   **Tell**: To say something.
*   **Tell apart**: To distinguish between two things/people.
</div>

---

### 1. Separable Phrasal Verbs
These are transitive verbs (they take an object). You can put the object **between** the verb and the particle or **after** it.

| Rule | Example |
| :--- | :--- |
| **Separated** | She **took** <ins>the rubbish</ins> **out**. |
| **Together** | She **took out** <ins>the rubbish</ins>. |

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Pronouns**: If the object is a pronoun (*it, them, me*), it **must** go in the middle.
> - ✅ *She **took it out**.*
> - ❌ *She took out it.*

---

### 2. Inseparable Phrasal Verbs
Some phrasal verbs cannot be separated. This happens if:
1.  The verb is **intransitive** (no object).
    *   *I **grew up** in London.*
2.  The particle is a **preposition** (the object must follow it).
    *   *I **came up with** <ins>a great idea</ins>.*

## Exercises

<div class="grammar-box" markdown="1">
#### Oral Practice
Answer these questions out loud:
1.  What time do you wake up during the week?
2.  Do you use an alarm to wake up?
3.  Do you get up immediately after you wake up?
4.  How do you feel when you first get up?
</div>

<div class="grammar-box" markdown="1">
#### Vocabulary Check
Explain what these phrasal verbs mean:
1.  The alarm **goes off**.
2.  I **wake up** on time.
3.  I **get up** at 4:45.
4.  We don't **go out** at all.
5.  I don't want to **give it up**.
</div>
