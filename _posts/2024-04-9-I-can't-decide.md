## 11B - Help! I can't decide!

### May vs. Might
**May** and **Might** are modal verbs used to talk about things that are possible but not certain. They can refer to both the present and the future.

<div class="grammar-box" markdown="1">
#### Structure
Like other modal verbs (*can, will, should*), they are followed by the base form of the verb (without "to").
<span class="formula">Subject + may/might (not) + Verb (Base Form)</span>
</div>

**Examples:**
*   *I **might go** to Australia this summer.* (Probability: ~50%).
*   *Bring your umbrella, it **might rain**.*
*   *He **might not be** at work today.* (Negative).

> [!NOTE]
> **Past Probability**: Use *might have + past participle*.
> - *She **might have been** asleep.*
> - *They **might have got** lost.*

---

### Noun Formation (Nominalisation)
We can form nouns from verbs or adjectives to create a more formal style.

| Verb / Adjective | Noun |
| :--- | :--- |
| Excite | **Excitement** |
| Grow | **Growth** |
| Invade | **Invasion** |
| Fly | **Flight** |
| Industrial | **Industrialisation** |
| Wide | **Width** |
