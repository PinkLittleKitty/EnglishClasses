### Obligations: Must vs. Have To
There are multiple ways of conveying obligations, but the most common are **Must** and **Have To**.

<div class="grammar-box" markdown="1">
#### 1. Must
**Must** is a modal verb, followed by the base form of the infinitive (Without "to").
<span class="formula">Subject + must + Verb (Base Form)</span>

*   ✅ *You **must go**.* (NOT: You must to go)
*   ✅ *He **must wait**.* (NOT: He must waits)
</div>

---

### What is "Must" used for?

<div class="grammar-box" markdown="1">
*   **Obligation/Duty**: Rules, laws, and regulations.
    *   *You **must** wear a seatbelt at all times.*
*   **Necessity**: Something essential.
    *   *Plants **must** have light and water to grow.*
*   **Deduction**: When you're sure something is true.
    *   *It **must** be cold outside (look at the snow).*
*   **Past Assumptions**: Using *Must + have + Past Participle*.
    *   *He **must have won** the lottery.*
*   **Strong Advice**: Recommendations you feel strongly about.
    *   *You **must** try these cupcakes.*
</div>

---

### Negatives: Mustn't
The negative of **Must** is **Mustn’t** (Must not).

> [!IMPORTANT]
> **Mustn’t** expresses **prohibition**. It is important that you do NOT do something.
> - *You **mustn't** smoke here.* (It is not allowed).

---

### Must vs. Have To

| Verb | Usage | Example |
| :--- | :--- | :--- |
| **Must** | The **speaker** decides it's necessary. | *"I **must** write a letter to John."* (I decided). |
| **Have To** | **External factor** / Someone else decided. | *"I **have to** write a letter."* (My boss told me). |

---

### Different Tenses
**Must** only exists in the present. For other tenses, we use **Have to**:

<div class="grammar-box" markdown="1">
*   **Past**: *Had to*
    *   *I **had to** pay my speeding ticket yesterday.*
*   **Future**: *Will have to* (or *I'll have to*)
    *   *I **will have to** leave work early tomorrow.*
</div>
